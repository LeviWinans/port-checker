Opening Your Eyes to The Possibilities What is Blepharoplasty? Blepharoplasty is a cosmetic procedure designed to improve the appearance of the upper or lower eyelids. Over time, the skin around the eyes begins to stretch, and fat pockets can create puffiness or bags. The blepharoplasty procedure gently corrects these changes by removing excess skin and, when needed, removing or repositioning excess fat. At our practice in Houston blepharoplasty is almost always done on an outpatient basis. Many patients are comfortable with local anesthesia, though sedation or general anesthesia may be an option depending on your needs. Because incisions are placed along the lash line or within the natural crease, they typically heal beautifully and leave little to no visible scarring.

See The Difference The Blepharoplasty Procedure Blepharoplasty is always customized, but here’s what you can generally expect: The entire procedure usually takes one to three hours, depending on the extent of correction. Thanks to careful surgical technique and anesthesia, most patients describe the experience as involving only mild discomfort. Upper eyelid surgery: We make a small incision in the natural crease of the upper lid, where it will be hidden once healed. Through this incision, we remove excess eyelid skin and sometimes small amounts of fat pockets to open up the eye and restore definition. Lower eyelid surgery: Incisions may be made just beneath the lash line or inside the lid (transconjunctival blepharoplasty). This allows us to remove or reposition fat and smooth the transition between the lower eyelid and cheek. If there’s significant loose skin, it can be gently trimmed and tightened. Quad blepharoplasty: Many patients choose to treat the upper and lower eyelids at the same time for a full rejuvenation.

Recovery After Eyelid Surgery Back at it in No Time Schedule Today Recovery from blepharoplasty is usually straightforward. You’ll go home the same day, and we’ll provide detailed aftercare instructions. Keeping your head elevated, using cold compresses, and applying eye drops help reduce swelling and irritation in the first week. It’s also normal to experience some swelling and bruising, blurry vision, or mild tearing at first. Stitches are typically removed within a week, and most people feel comfortable returning to work and normal activities within 7–10 days. Light exercise can usually resume after two weeks. We recommend dark sunglasses outdoors until your eyelids are fully healed to protect your delicate skin. Take a Break Aftercare Tips Following aftercare closely ensures the best results. Use your prescribed eye drops, avoid rubbing your eyes, and hold off on makeup and contact lenses until cleared at your follow-up visit. Some patients combine blepharoplasty with injectable treatments or other facial procedures later to extend their results, but many enjoy the outcome of surgery for a decade or longer.

Blepharoplasty Frequently Asked Questions Can blepharoplasty improve my vision, or is it just cosmetic? Yes, blepharoplasty can improve vision when excess eyelid skin droops into your line of sight. By removing that skin, patients often notice clearer upper vision. For others, it’s mainly a cosmetic procedure to restore a brighter, more youthful look. How long does recovery take, and when will I look “normal” again? Most patients see swelling and bruising fade within 7–10 days. By this point, you can usually return to work and social activities. Final results and incision refinement continue improving over several weeks. Will I have scars after eyelid surgery? Incisions are carefully placed in the natural crease of the upper eyelid or just below the lash line for lower lids. With proper healing, scars fade quickly and become barely visible. Many patients find they are practically undetectable. Am I a good candidate for upper, lower, or both eyelids? If you have drooping upper eyelids, puffy under-eye bags, or loose lower eyelid skin, you may be a candidate. Some benefit from upper eyelid blepharoplasty, some from lower, and others from combining both for the best balance. What are the risks of blepharoplasty? Risks are rare but may include temporary swelling, bruising, dry eyes, or blurry vision. Choosing a board-certified surgeon and following aftercare closely helps minimize complications and ensures safe, natural-looking results.
