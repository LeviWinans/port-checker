What Is a Chin Implant? A chin implant is a facial plastic surgery procedure that places a carefully selected implant over the chin bone to increase projection, improve contour, and bring the chin into better proportion with the nose, jawline, and surrounding facial features. Also known as chin augmentation or implant-based mentoplasty, the procedure is designed for patients with an underprojected or disproportionately small chin. The implant adds structure beneath the soft tissue rather than simply filling the skin. This allows the surgeon to make a measured, lasting change to the profile. Chin implants come in different sizes and shapes. Some primarily increase forward projection, while others extend along the jaw to create a wider or more defined contour. Selection depends on the patient’s bone structure, facial width, profile, and aesthetic goals. The best result is rarely about creating the largest possible chin. It is about finding the degree of projection that brings the rest of the face into focus.

What Aesthetic Issues Do Chin Implants Address? The chin plays a quiet but influential role in facial balance. When it sits too far back, the nose can appear more prominent, the jawline may look less defined, and the transition between the face and neck can feel softer. A chin implant cannot correct every lower-face concern. It does not remove fat beneath the chin, tighten loose neck skin, or correct a significant bite problem. Patients with jaw misalignment, functional concerns, or more complex skeletal deficiencies may need evaluation for genioplasty or orthognathic surgery. Precise diagnosis matters. A soft jawline may come from a recessed chin, excess submental fat, skin laxity, or a combination of all three. Chin implant surgery may address: A weak or receding chin Limited forward chin projection A disproportionately small chin Imbalance between the nose and chin A lower face that appears short Poor definition where the chin meets the jawline Mild chin asymmetry A profile that lacks structural balance Congenital underdevelopment of the chin

Am I a Candidate for a Chin Implant? Schedule Today Chin implants are generally suited to healthy adults whose facial growth is complete and whose primary concern is limited chin projection. During consultation, your surgeon will study your face from the front, profile, and three-quarter views. Chin height, width, projection, symmetry, dental alignment, and the relationship between the chin and neck all influence the recommendation. An implant adds volume over the bone. It cannot move the jaw, substantially shorten or lengthen the chin, or correct an abnormal bite. Sliding genioplasty may be more appropriate when the chin needs significant horizontal or vertical repositioning. Orthognathic surgery may be required when the underlying concern involves jaw alignment or dental function. Chin filler can be considered when a patient wants a temporary, nonsurgical change or needs only a small degree of projection. Patients with significant submental fullness or neck laxity may benefit from combining chin augmentation with neck liposuction, a neck lift, or another facial-contouring procedure. You may be a good candidate if you: Have a weak, recessed, or disproportionately small chin Want stronger lower-face definition Are physically healthy and at a stable weight Do not smoke or can stop nicotine as directed Have realistic expectations about the degree of correction Understand the procedure’s risks and recovery Do not have an active dental or oral infection Want a long-lasting alternative to injectable chin enhancement Try the Virtual Consultation Tool Use our interactive virtual consultation tool to explore the plastic surgery procedures that might best fit your unique concerns. How Should I Prepare for Chin Implant Surgery? Preparation begins with a facial assessment and a clear understanding of what the implant can achieve. 1. Complete your consultation. Your surgeon will review your profile, facial proportions, medical history, previous procedures, and desired degree of projection. 2. Discuss dental and oral health. Report recent dental procedures, gum disease, oral infections, implants, or planned dental work. This is particularly important if an incision inside the mouth is being considered. 3. Review medications and supplements. Certain medications and supplements may increase bleeding risk. Do not change a prescription unless your prescribing clinician and surgical team provide coordinated instructions. 4. Stop nicotine as directed. Smoking, vaping, and other nicotine products can restrict circulation and interfere with incision healing. 5. Arrange transportation and support. You will need someone to drive you home. Depending on the anesthesia plan, your surgeon may also recommend having an adult stay with you during the first night. 6. Prepare suitable foods and recovery supplies. Patients having an intraoral incision may need soft foods and a prescribed mouth rinse. Keep recommended dressings, medications, and cold compresses readily available. The Chin Implant Procedure Chin implant surgery is performed as an outpatient procedure in Heights Plastic Surgery’s private, on-site surgical center. It usually takes approximately 45 minutes to one hour, although timing can vary when another facial procedure is included. Local anesthesia, intravenous sedation, or general anesthesia may be used. Your surgeon will recommend the appropriate option based on the surgical plan, incision location, combined procedures, and your comfort. An incision is created either beneath the chin or inside the lower lip. Each approach has distinct considerations. Incision Beneath the Chin A small submental incision gives the surgeon direct access to the chin bone. The resulting scar is positioned in a naturally shadowed area beneath the chin. Incision Inside the Mouth An intraoral incision avoids an external scar but requires careful oral hygiene during recovery. Temporary dietary modifications and a prescribed mouth rinse may be recommended. Through the incision, the surgeon creates a precise pocket directly over the chin bone. The selected implant is placed and checked for projection, symmetry, and stability before the incision is closed. Implant position is critical. A pocket that is too large may allow movement, while an implant that does not match the anatomy can look or feel unbalanced. The surgical plan accounts for facial width, chin shape, and the contours extending toward the jaw. Possible risks include bleeding, infection, asymmetry, numbness, implant displacement, prolonged swelling, unfavorable scarring, capsular contracture, and the possibility of revision surgery.

Chin Implant Results Greater chin projection is visible immediately, but swelling can make the early result look fuller or less defined than expected. The implant may also feel firm or unfamiliar while the surrounding tissues heal. During the first few weeks, swelling gradually decreases and the lower face begins to look more balanced. Residual firmness and subtle swelling can take several months to resolve fully. A solid chin implant is designed to provide a lasting structural change. It does not naturally dissolve like injectable filler. Revision or removal may still become necessary if the implant shifts, becomes infected, creates an unwanted contour, or no longer matches the patient’s preferences. Results depend on implant selection, surgical placement, facial anatomy, and healing. Perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed, and some natural difference between the two sides of the face will remain.

Recovery After Chin Implant Surgery Most patients plan for approximately one to two weeks of visible recovery. Swelling, bruising, soreness, tightness, and temporary numbness are common during the early phase. The First 48 Hours Swelling is usually most noticeable during the first few days. Keeping the head elevated and using cold compresses as directed can improve comfort. A supportive dressing may be placed around the chin. The First Week Bruising and swelling begin to settle. Many patients feel comfortable returning to desk work after about one week, although the chin may still look slightly full. Talking and smiling can feel tight at first. Patients with an incision inside the mouth may be asked to follow a soft or liquid diet temporarily and use a special mouth rinse. The Following Weeks The implant settles as the tissues heal around it. Strenuous exercise, contact sports, and activities that place pressure on the chin remain restricted until the surgical team provides clearance. Final refinement develops gradually. The chin should look increasingly integrated with the face as swelling resolves. Aftercare Following Chin Implant Surgery Protecting the implant and incision during early healing helps reduce avoidable complications. Call the office if you develop increasing redness, worsening pain, fever, drainage, an opening along the incision, or a sudden change in implant position. Chest pain, shortness of breath, or an unusual heartbeat requires immediate medical attention. Patients are generally advised to: Keep the head elevated while resting Wear dressings or support garments as directed Use cold compresses only according to postoperative instructions Take medications as prescribed Avoid touching, pressing, or sleeping directly on the chin Maintain careful oral hygiene after an intraoral incision Follow temporary dietary instructions Postpone strenuous exercise until cleared Avoid nicotine throughout recovery Attend every scheduled follow-up visit

Chin Implants Frequently Asked Questions What is a chin implant? Improving someone’s physical appearance is possible thanks to the numerous advances in cosmetic science. One way to boost your physical appearance is to undergo chin implant surgery. The shape of your chin enhances your overall facial features and balances the appearance of your nose. Mentoplasty, otherwise known as chin surgery, can alter the shape of your chin by using a special implant to add shape to the chin, increase the projection of the chin, or augment the underlying bone of the chin. For smaller changes, an implant may be ideal. However, for more dramatic change, cutting, moving, and placing a titanium plate to hold the bone in a new position is often required. How much does a chin implant cost in Houston? The average cost of your chin implant surgery in Houston typically ranges between $4,500 and $6,000. The total cost for your Houston chin implant surgery will depend on factors such as your surgeon’s fee, the location of your procedure, and other miscellaneous medical expenses. At Heights Plastic Surgery, we accept cash and all major credit cards. We are also happy to offer financing through CareCredit® for patients. Because a chin implant is often more about cosmetic appearance and is less of a medical necessity, it is not covered by most insurance plans. How does chin surgery work? Before your chin implant surgery begins, we will administer a general anesthetic. This is to ensure you are as comfortable and pain-free as possible throughout the entirety of your procedure. Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks will then make a small incision on the inside of the mouth between the bottom lip and gums, or on the underside of the chin. Through this incision, we will carefully place the soft, chin implant. Once the chin implant is in its correct position, Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks will stitch up your incisions and bandage your surgical area. The entire surgical procedure typically takes between 30 minutes and one hours to complete. What should I expect when recovering from a chin surgery? Following a chin implant Houston patients often experience swelling and bruising around the treated area for the first seven to 10 days following their chin implant surgery. Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks may provide you with prescription medication that can help manage your pain. In addition to icing consistently for the first 48 hours following your procedure, Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks and their team will ask that you keep your head elevated when laying down for the first two weeks following your chin implant procedure. Patients are typically cleared to return to work and regular daily activities about one week after their chin implant surgery. While you will be able to see some results of your chin implant surgery immediately, ultimate results will appear once all incisions are healed and swelling subsides. Am I a good candidate for a chin implant? At Heights Plastic Surgery, good candidates for a chin implant experience the following concerns: A weak, or receding chin A disproportionately small chin Wish to bring balance to facial features Ideal candidates for a chin implant are in good general health and have realistic expectations of their potential results.
