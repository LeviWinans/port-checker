View All Before & Afters What is Breast Fat Transfer? Breast Fat Transfer is a surgical procedure that harvests fat from areas of excess, typically the abdomen, flanks, or thighs, and reinjects it into the breasts to increase volume. This autologous method allows for a modest size increase and improved contour without the use of synthetic implants. What Aesthetic Issues Does Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation Address? Many patients are satisfied with their overall shape but feel their breasts have “deflated” due to weight loss, breastfeeding, or natural aging. Implants can feel like too large a commitment for those seeking only a subtle change. This procedure specifically targets: Volume Loss: Restoring the upper pole fullness that often disappears after pregnancy. Asymmetry: Correcting significant size differences between the left and right breast by precisely adding volume to the smaller side. Cleavage Definition: improving the medial definition without the rigid look of an implant. Implant Edge Camouflage: For patients with existing implants, fat can be layered over the edges to hide rippling or visible transitions. Benefits of Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation The primary advantage of this technique is the “dual benefit”: you slim a problem area while enhancing the chest. Because the filler is your own living tissue, the breast moves, feels, and ages exactly like natural breast tissue. No Foreign Body: Zero risk of implant rupture, capsular contracture, or breast implant illness (BII). Body Contouring: The donor site (abdomen, thighs, or flanks) is sculpted via liposuction, resulting in a slimmer overall silhouette. Natural Aesthetics: The results are soft and warm to the touch, indistinguishable from native tissue. Minimal Scarring: The procedure utilizes tiny access points for the cannula, leaving scars that are often less than 3mm long. Candidates for Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation is not a universal substitute for implants. It relies on the availability of donor fat and realistic sizing goals. Fat Availability: You must have enough subcutaneous fat in a donor area to harvest. Very lean athletes may not qualify. Modest Goals: This technique typically achieves a 0.5 to 1 cup size increase. If you want a significant jump (e.g., A cup to C cup), implants are likely the better tool. Skin Quality: Patients with extreme skin laxity (sagging) may require a breast lift in conjunction with the transfer to reshape the breast mound. Non-Smokers: Nicotine kills fat cells. You must be nicotine-free for at least 6 weeks prior to surgery to ensure the grafted fat survives. How to Prepare for Your Procedure Preparation for Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation is focused on maximizing the survival rate of the transferred fat cells. Weight Stability: You should be at a stable goal weight. Gaining or losing significant weight after surgery will alter your results. Nutrition: Hydration and high-protein intake in the weeks leading up to surgery create a healthy environment for the new grafts. Garment Planning: You will need specific compression garments for the donor site (liposuction area) and a loose, non-compressive surgical bra for the chest. Support System: Arrange for a ride home and someone to help with basic tasks for the first 24–48 hours. The Procedure The Harvesting Phase Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks begins with liposuction of the agreed-upon donor sites. We use a gentle, low-pressure aspiration technique to preserve the viability of the fat cells. The collected fat is then purified to remove oil, blood, and water, leaving only healthy, concentrated adipose tissue. The Injection Phase This is the artistic portion of the surgery. We use fine cannulas to deposit the fat in tiny droplets throughout multiple layers of the breast tissue: under the skin, into the gland, and over the muscle. This “micro-droplet” technique ensures that every fat cell has a blood supply, which is critical for its survival. Results: What to Expect Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation results are dynamic. Immediate Post-Op: You will look larger than the final result due to swelling. This is normal. The “Settle” (Weeks 6–12): The body will naturally reabsorb about 30–40% of the transferred fat. The remaining 60–70% establishes a blood supply and becomes permanent. Final Result: By month three, the size you see is your permanent baseline. These fat cells will behave like any other fat in your body—if you gain weight, your breasts will get larger; if you lose weight, they may shrink. Recovery Timeline Recovery after Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation is unique because you are healing two different areas: the donor site and the breasts. Days 1–3: Expect soreness and bruising at the liposuction site (similar to a heavy workout). The breasts may feel tight, but are generally less painful than the donor area. Week 1: You can typically return to a desk job. You must avoid any pressure on the chest—no sleeping on your stomach and no tight sports bras. Week 3–4: You can resume light cardio. Week 6: Full activity, including heavy lifting and chest exercises, is usually cleared. Aftercare for Fat Survival Post-operative care for Fat Transfer Breast Augmentation contradicts standard advice. No Ice on the Breasts: Cold restricts blood vessels and can kill the fragile new fat cells. Keep the chest warm. Avoid Compression: Unlike implants, which we sometimes strap down, fat grafts need space. You will wear a soft, loose bra or camisole to protect the area without squishing it. Donor Site Compression: Conversely, you will wear tight compression on the liposuction areas (abdomen/thighs) to minimize swelling and smooth the contour.
