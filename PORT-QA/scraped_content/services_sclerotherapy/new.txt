See Results What Is Sclerotherapy? Sclerotherapy is a minimally invasive vein treatment that uses a precisely placed injectable solution to close spider veins and selected small varicose veins. Blood naturally redirects through healthier vessels, while the treated vein gradually fades from view. Sclerotherapy has remained one of the most established treatments for visible leg veins because it addresses the vessel itself. There are no surgical incisions, and anesthesia is generally unnecessary. Most appointments take between 15 minutes and one hour, depending on the number, size, and location of the veins being treated. At Studio Essex at Heights Plastic Surgery, each sclerotherapy plan begins with an assessment of the visible vessels and any related symptoms. Larger, painful, or more complex varicose veins may require diagnostic ultrasound or evaluation by a vascular specialist before cosmetic treatment is considered.

What Aesthetic Issues Does Sclerotherapy Address? Visible veins can change the way the legs look even when the surrounding skin is smooth and healthy. They may appear as isolated lines, branching patterns, or clusters that become increasingly noticeable with age, pregnancy, hormonal changes, genetics, or long periods spent standing. The treatment is best suited to vessels that can be safely closed without affecting healthy circulation. Sclerotherapy does not prevent every future vein from forming, and it may not be appropriate for large varicose veins caused by deeper venous insufficiency. Am I a Candidate for Sclerotherapy? Schedule Today Sclerotherapy may be appropriate for adults who are bothered by visible spider veins or selected small varicose veins and want an in-office treatment with minimal interruption to their schedule. Good candidates typically: Are in good general health Have spider veins, reticular veins, or small varicose veins Experience mild discomfort related to suitable superficial veins Can walk normally after treatment Have realistic expectations about gradual fading Understand that a series may be needed Are willing to wear compression garments when instructed Can follow temporary exercise, heat, and sun restrictions Who May Not Be a Good Fit? Sclerotherapy is generally postponed during pregnancy and breastfeeding. It may also be inappropriate for patients with an active blood clot, significant mobility limitations, an allergy to the proposed sclerosant, or certain vascular conditions. A previous blood clot does not automatically rule out treatment, but it requires a careful medical review. Patients with substantial swelling, skin ulcers, bleeding veins, warmth, unexplained leg pain, or large bulging veins may need diagnostic testing or a vascular evaluation before proceeding. The consultation is meant to determine which vessels can be treated safely and which concerns deserve a different level of care. How Should I Prepare for Sclerotherapy? Preparation is straightforward, but a thoughtful medical review remains essential. Discuss your health history Tell your provider about previous blood clots, vascular disease, heart conditions, pregnancy, allergies, recent illnesses, and earlier vein treatments. Review medications and supplements Certain blood thinners, anti-inflammatory medications, antibiotics, supplements, and prescription drugs may affect treatment or bruising. Do not stop a prescribed medication unless the clinician managing it and your sclerotherapy provider have given clear instructions. Ask whether imaging is appropriate Diagnostic ultrasound may be recommended when veins are larger, painful, associated with swelling, or suspected to involve deeper circulation. Leave the skin undisturbed Avoid shaving the treatment area or applying lotion immediately before your appointment unless your provider advises otherwise. Wear comfortable clothing Loose clothing makes it easier to access the treatment area and accommodate compression stockings or bandages afterward. Bring prescribed compression garments If your provider has recommended a particular garment, bring it to your appointment so its fit and compression level can be confirmed. What Is a Sclerotherapy Appointment Like? Sclerotherapy is performed in the office. You will usually lie on your back with the treatment area exposed and, depending on the location, your legs slightly elevated. Your provider first cleans the skin and studies the vein pattern. A very fine needle is then used to place a liquid or foam sclerosant directly into the selected vessel. The solution irritates the vein’s inner lining, causing the vessel to close. Blood shifts into healthier veins, and the treated vessel is gradually absorbed by the body. The number of injections depends on the number and size of the veins being treated. Pressure or gentle massage may be applied after each injection, followed by a pad, bandage, or compression garment. Most patients describe a quick pinch followed by mild stinging, warmth, or brief cramping. Significant pain should be reported immediately because it may indicate that the solution has moved outside the intended vessel. The appointment generally takes 15 minutes to one hour. Patients may be observed briefly afterward and are usually encouraged to walk before leaving.

When Will I See Sclerotherapy Results? Sclerotherapy results develop gradually. The treated veins may look darker, more prominent, or slightly raised before they begin to fade. Bruising can also temporarily obscure early improvement. Small spider veins often respond within three to six weeks. Larger treated veins may require three to four months to fade. The exact timeline depends on vessel size, location, circulation, skin tone, and the body’s healing response. Some veins respond after one appointment. Others require additional injections. Sclerotherapy may substantially reduce a visible vessel without making it disappear completely, so expectations should be based on the individual vein pattern rather than a promise of perfect clearance. Once a vein has responded successfully, it typically remains closed. Treatment does not prevent new visible veins from developing later.

Recovery After Sclerotherapy Sclerotherapy has little formal downtime, and most patients return to work and ordinary daily activities on the same day. Walking is commonly encouraged shortly after treatment to support circulation. Immediate Downtime Mild stinging, warmth, redness, swelling, or tenderness may appear around the injection sites. Small bruises are also common. These effects usually improve over several days, although bruising may take longer to clear. Social Downtime There is usually no required social recovery. Compression stockings, bruising, temporary discoloration, or firm areas along a treated vein may remain visible, so timing treatment well ahead of a vacation or special event is wise. Exercise and Heat Light walking is generally encouraged. Strenuous workouts, heavy lifting, hot baths, saunas, whirlpools, and other intense heat exposure may need to be avoided temporarily. Your provider will give instructions based on the vessels treated and your medical history. Skin Changes Temporary brown lines or spots can develop along an injected vessel. These often fade over time, but some pigmentation can persist longer. Careful sun protection is particularly important while the treatment area heals.

Sclerotherapy Aftercare Schedule Today Compression and movement are two of the most important parts of sclerotherapy aftercare. Your provider will specify how long compression stockings or wraps should be worn, since recommendations vary with vessel size and treatment technique. Contact the office if you develop increasing pain, significant swelling, skin ulceration, spreading redness, or other unexpected changes. Sudden leg swelling, chest pain, trouble breathing, dizziness, coughing up blood, or new neurological symptoms require immediate medical attention. Sclerotherapy Frequently Asked Questions What is sclerotherapy? Sclerotherapy is a minimally invasive treatment that reduces the physical discomfort and appearance of varicose veins, or the swollen, raised and discolored veins that appear on the surface of the skin. While sclerotherapy is typically used to treat varicose veins and spider veins on the legs and feet, this treatment can also be used to reduce discoloration and improve comfort in the calves, ankles, thighs, and face. Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks simply injects a solution of sclerosing agents into the affected veins to produce your desired results. How much does sclerotherapy cost in Houston? The average cost of sclerotherapy in Houston typically starts at $600. The total cost for your sclerotherapy treatment will depend on factors such as your surgeon’s fee, the location of your procedure, and other miscellaneous medical expenses. At Heights Plastic Surgery, we accept cash and all major credit cards. We are also happy to offer financing through CareCredit® for patients. Because sclerotherapy treatments are typically more about cosmetic appearance and is less of a medical necessity, it is not covered by most insurance plans. How is sclerotherapy performed? Before your sclerotherapy begins, Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks will prepare your skin by thoroughly cleaning it and confirming the areas which you would like to be treated. She will then begin to inject your desired varicose veins and spider veins with the sclerosing agents. These sclerosing agents will close off the entrance of the vein, redirecting the blood into healthier, more viable veins in the area. Each treatment session typically takes anywhere between 15 minutes and an hour to complete, depending on the size of the treated area. Is there downtime after sclerotherapy? Because sclerotherapy is a minimally invasive treatment, there is no formal downtime or recovery period associated with the procedure. Most patients are able to return to their normal daily activities, including work, following their sclerotherapy treatment in Houston. Following sclerotherapy treatments, it is normal to experience swelling, bruising, and general discomfort in the treated areas. This typically resolves on its own within a few days of treatment. While many patients may start to see the results of their sclerotherapy treatments after just one session, Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks may recommend up to a series of three to five treatments spaced a few weeks apart to achieve ultimate results.
