What Is a Lower Body Lift? A lower body lift, also called a belt lipectomy, is a circumferential body contouring procedure that removes excess skin and selected fat from the abdomen, waist, hips, lower back, buttocks, and outer thighs while lifting and supporting the remaining tissues. This is one of the most comprehensive procedures available after significant weight loss. Loose tissue often extends around the body rather than staying confined to the abdomen. A lower body lift addresses that full circumference, creating a smoother transition through the waist, flanks, lower back, and upper legs. At Heights Plastic Surgery, the surgical plan is tailored to the patient’s anatomy. Some patients need a complete belt lipectomy. Others benefit from a more selective combination of abdominoplasty, buttock lifting, lateral thigh lifting, panniculectomy, or liposuction. Dr. Cara Downey and Dr. Jamal Bullocks evaluate where the skin has descended, how the tissues connect, and which combination will produce the most balanced result.

How to Prepare for a Lower Body Lift Preparation is especially important for an operation of this scale. Your surgical team will provide instructions based on your health, medications, anatomy, and planned combination of procedures. Complete your medical evaluation Preoperative testing may include laboratory work, medical clearance, and a review of any conditions that could affect anesthesia, circulation, or healing. Protect your nutritional health Adequate protein, vitamins, and hydration help support tissue repair. Patients who have undergone bariatric surgery may need additional nutritional testing before they are cleared. Keep your weight stable Reaching a sustainable weight before surgery creates a more predictable contour and helps preserve the result. Stop nicotine as directed Nicotine restricts blood flow and can significantly increase the risk of delayed healing, wound separation, and tissue loss. Review medications and supplements Your surgeon may ask you to adjust products that increase bleeding risk. Do not change a prescription without guidance from the prescribing clinician and your surgical team. Prepare your home Arrange a comfortable recovery space, easy access to necessities, and help with meals, household responsibilities, children, and pets. Plan transportation and support You will need someone to drive you home and remain with you during the early recovery period. The Lower Body Lift Procedure A lower body lift at Heights Plastic Surgery is performed under general anesthesia in the practice’s private, on-site surgical center. The operation generally takes four to eight hours, depending on the extent of correction and the procedures included. The incision usually follows a low, circumferential pattern around the torso. Its precise position is planned with undergarments and swimwear in mind, although the final scar pattern depends on the location and quantity of skin being removed. Through this incision, the surgeon removes a belt of excess skin and selected fat. The abdominal portion may include repair of separated or weakened muscles when appropriate. The remaining tissues are elevated and redraped to improve the waist, lower back, buttocks, and outer thighs. Continue Liposuction may be added selectively to refine particular areas, but it is used carefully. Preserving a healthy blood supply to the skin is a priority during an operation involving extensive tissue movement. Deep sutures support the newly shaped contours, and the skin is closed in layers. Temporary drains may be placed to manage fluid during early healing. A compression garment is generally applied before the patient leaves the surgical facility. Your consultation includes a detailed discussion of the procedure’s risks, including bleeding, infection, fluid accumulation, delayed wound healing, numbness, asymmetry, blood clots, and unfavorable scarring.

Lower Body Lift Results The change in body contour is visible immediately, although swelling and compression garments initially obscure some of the detail. The abdomen typically appears flatter, the waist more defined, and the buttocks and outer thighs better supported. During the first six weeks, swelling decreases and the tissues begin adapting to their new position. Further refinement continues over the following months. Scars often require a year or longer to flatten, soften, and fade, with complete maturation sometimes taking up to two years. Lower body lift results can be long-lasting when patients maintain a stable weight and healthy routine. The body will continue to age naturally, and future pregnancy or significant weight fluctuations can stretch the remaining skin. A circumferential scar is an unavoidable trade-off. Heights Plastic Surgery plans its position carefully and provides scar-care guidance as healing progresses. Most patients considering this procedure decide that the improvement in contour is worth the scar required to achieve it.

Recovery After a Lower Body Lift Lower body lift recovery requires time, planning, and reliable support. This is an extensive operation, and patients should expect a more involved recovery than they would after liposuction or a limited tummy tuck. The first few days Swelling, bruising, tightness, soreness, and difficulty standing fully upright are expected. Dressings, drains, and a compression garment may be used. Short, assisted walks are usually encouraged as directed to support circulation, but movement should remain controlled. The first two weeks Patients gradually become more independent, although help with meals, bathing, garments, and household responsibilities may still be necessary. Follow-up appointments allow the surgical team to monitor the incisions and remove drains when output has decreased appropriately. Weeks three and four Mobility generally improves, but fatigue can linger. Many patients need approximately four weeks away from work, driving, and normal obligations. Physically demanding jobs may require additional time. Weeks six through eight Light exercise and routine household activity may resume once the surgeon confirms that healing is progressing safely. Heavy lifting, high-impact exercise, and movements that place tension on the incision remain restricted until clearance is given. Recovery is individualized. The extent of surgery, baseline health, healing response, and any additional procedures can all affect the timeline.

Aftercare Following a Lower Body Lift Careful aftercare protects the incision, reduces avoidable complications, and supports a smoother recovery. Contact the surgical team promptly if you notice worsening redness, increasing pain, fever, unusual drainage, sudden swelling, or an opening along the incision. Chest pain, shortness of breath, an unusual heartbeat, or one-sided calf pain requires immediate medical attention. The early recovery can feel demanding, but it is temporary. Consistent aftercare gives the body the best opportunity to heal safely and settle into its new contour. Patients are generally advised to: Wear compression garments as directed Keep incisions clean and dry according to postoperative instructions Measure and record drain output Take medications only as prescribed Walk briefly and regularly as instructed Avoid lifting, pulling, and strenuous activity Maintain adequate nutrition and hydration Avoid nicotine throughout recovery Attend every scheduled follow-up appointment Protect healed scars from sun exposure Begin scar therapy only after receiving clearance

Lower Body Lift Frequently Asked Questions What is a lower body lift? After going through a major weight loss, many patients come in complaining about the excess skin left behind. While home remedies, topical creams, and non-surgical treatments may help to an extent, large amounts of loose skin requires a more invasive, but permanent technique. Sagging or loose skin in the abdomen, hips, buttocks, and thighs can be removed with a lower body lift. This powerful body contouring procedure is used by board-certified plastic surgeons, Dr. Cara Downey and Dr. Jamal Bullocks, to give the lower body the younger look and feel that patients deserve after accomplishing their major weight loss. How much does a lower body lift cost in Houston? The average cost of your lower body lift in Houston typically ranges between $8,000 and $15,000. The total cost for your lower body lift will depend on factors such as your surgeon’s fee, the location of your procedure, and other miscellaneous medical expenses. At Heights Plastic Surgery, we accept cash and all major credit cards. We are also happy to offer financing through CareCredit® for patients. Because a lower body lift is typically more about cosmetic appearance and is less of a medical necessity, it is not covered by most insurance plans. Am I a good candidate for a lower body lift? At Heights Plastic Surgery, Houston lower body lift candidates typically experience the following concerns: Loose skin around the midsection Excess skin and fat around the thighs Sagging and loose skin around the buttocks Ideal candidates for a lower body lift are in good general health and have realistic expectations of their potential results. Patients will be required to be near or at their ideal body weight. The best way to determine if you are a good candidate for a lower body lift is to schedule your private consultation with Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks. What techniques do you use during a lower body lift? A lower body lift is an outpatient procedure that typically takes about 4 to 8 hours to complete. To begin, a general anesthetic will be administered to ensure you are comfortable and pain-free throughout your surgery. Houston plastic surgeons Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks will then perform a combination of any of the following techniques. The surgical techniques Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks will use during your lower body lift will be discussed while developing your personalized treatment plan during your one-on-one consultation. Belt Lipectomy. This technique is the “complete” lower body lift procedure, which involves a circumferential incision along with your entire waist and along your outer thigh. The extent of these incisions enables a powerful lift on the entirety of the lower body, getting rid of fat, tightening muscles, and removing loose skin for a more contoured figure. Tummy Tuck. A lower body lift performed specifically on the abdomen, hips, thighs, and buttocks. The main purpose of a tummy tuck is to tighten the abdominal wall and remove stubborn fat and skin from the stomach. This restores abdominal strength in addition to rejuvenating the mid-section. Lateral Thigh Lift. This technique removes excess fat from the other thigh, often performed in conjunction with liposuction to the area. Buttock Lift. During a buttock lift, we remove excess skin and fat from the buttocks and reposition remaining skin for a smoother, more contoured look. Panniculectomy. Panniculectomy specifically targets loose skin above the pubic area. What can I expect during recovery for a lower body lift? For the first 24 hours following your lower body lift, you will be fitted with drains and compression garments. These will aid in reducing your swelling and promoting your healing. Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks will also provide you with prescription medication that can help manage your pain. You will be able to resume taking full-body showers about two days following your lower body lift. After about 2 weeks, Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks and their team will encourage you to practice walking in your new body with the assistance of a friend or family member. Dr. Downey or Dr. Bullocks asks that you refrain from driving and working for at least 4 weeks, or until it is no longer painful to move and wear a seat belt. Most normal daily tasks, such as doing chores around the house and participating in light exercise, may continue after about six weeks. At 6 to 8 weeks, your scars should begin to fade, and will gradually improve over time. While you will see most results of your lower body lift after just 6 weeks, ultimate results will not become apparent until all swelling subsides and the skin has settled into its new position.
